# 1. One-page recommendation: who pays, for what, and why it might fail

Prepared 3 October 2026. Status: **recommendation, not validated.** Nothing here is customer evidence.

## 1.1 The short answer

Keep the board-minutes niche, but narrow the buyer and change the pitch.

**Buyer:** a Botswana company-secretarial practice (stand-alone secretarial firm, or the secretarial desk of a law or accounting firm) that prepares formal minutes for **several client companies that do not run a board portal**. Target the person who signs off the minutes and controls the practice's spend: the practice head, partner or senior company secretary.

**Problem:** after each meeting someone spends hours turning notes or a recording into minutes the chair will sign, then chases actions by email. Each client wants its own format. Errors in decisions, votes and who-said-what are embarrassing and can become evidence later.

**Offer:** "Send us the recording and the agenda. Within two business days you get draft minutes in your own Word template. Every decision, resolution and action is linked to the exact moment in the recording, and anything the recording does not establish is flagged, not invented." Sold first as a **paid 8-week pilot, delivered as a disclosed assisted service** (software plus a human operator), not as finished software.

## 1.2 What changed since the handover

A quick check on 3 October 2026 (search results only; vendor pages were blocked from this environment, so verify before relying on them) shows that board-minutes AI already exists:

| Product | What it appears to offer |
|---|---|
| BoardPro AI Minutes | Drafts minutes from a transcript against the board's agenda: notes, actions, decisions. Inside the BoardPro portal. |
| Diligent Smart Minutes, OnBoard, Convene, BoardEffect, Board Intelligence | AI minute drafting inside enterprise board portals. |
| Boardable | AI meeting-minutes feature, aimed at nonprofits. |
| AgendaWorx (inSite Connect, South Africa) | Board-pack platform. Recent sponsored press says it turns agenda plus transcript into minutes. Users include Momentum, Sanlam, Liberty. |

**What this means:** "AI drafts board minutes" is no longer a differentiator. It is a feature that portals give away. Our only defensible angles are:

1. **Portal-free, multi-client.** Portals are priced per board and assume each client buys a portal. A secretarial practice with 10 to 30 small clients usually works in Word and email. We serve the practice, not each board.
2. **Evidence and "not established".** Minutes the secretary can defend: every material statement linked to the recording, and no invented votes, attendance or deadlines.
3. **Botswana data handling.** Our searches indicate the Data Protection Act, 2024 (in force 14 January 2025) restricts transfers of personal data out of Botswana and requires a complete copy to be kept in Botswana. **Unverified: check the Act on Lexis+ before relying on this.** If correct, this is both a sales point (offshore portals may struggle with it) and a cost and architecture constraint for us.
4. **Local formats and, later, English/Setswana switching.** Research topic only, not a promise.

## 1.3 Why this niche might succeed

1. Recurring work: every client has quarterly boards, AGMs and committees. Repeat use is built in.
2. Effort is measurable: hours per set of minutes can be timed before and after.
3. The founder sits in this market and can get introductions.
4. Small practices are unlikely to buy a per-board portal for every client.

## 1.4 Why it might fail

1. **Free substitutes are good enough.** Copilot in Teams, Otter or Fathom transcripts plus a Word template may already save most of the time, at no extra cost.
2. **The market is small.** Botswana may have only a few dozen practices of the right size. That can fund a lifestyle business, not a large one, unless the model travels to SADC.
3. **Secretaries don't record.** Many boards may refuse to record at all, or record without a usable audio feed. No recording means no product.
4. **Trust barrier.** Chairs and directors may not allow board audio to go to a start-up, especially offshore.
5. **Assisted service never becomes software.** If editing still takes a human two hours per meeting, margins do not work at US$249 a month.
6. **Founder time and conflict.** Selling to the firm's own clients or peers raises confidentiality and professional-conduct questions. Keep the venture separate from client files.

## 1.5 The test

In 30 days, interview 12 qualified buyers and try to sell three paid pilots. If buyers will not pay a meaningful amount to try it, change the segment or stop. The gates are in `06-gates-decisions-assumptions.md`.
