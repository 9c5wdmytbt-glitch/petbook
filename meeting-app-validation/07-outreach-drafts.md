# 7. Outreach drafts. NOT SENT

Send these only from your own account, to people you know or have been introduced to. Edit freely. They are deliberately short, and they ask for advice, not a sale.

## 7.1 Direct note to a peer or practice head (WhatsApp or email)

> Subject: 30 minutes of your experience on board minutes
>
> Dear [Name],
>
> Separately from the firm, I'm looking at a venture around how board and committee minutes get prepared. Before building anything I want to understand how practices like yours actually do it today: the time, the headaches, what you've tried.
>
> Could I have 30 to 40 minutes of your time in the next two weeks? I'm not selling anything at this stage, and I don't need any client information, just how the process works.
>
> [Day] or [day] suit me. Happy to come to you.
>
> Regards,
> [Name]

## 7.2 Asking for an introduction

> Dear [Name],
>
> I'm researching how company secretaries prepare formal minutes, for a venture I'm exploring separately from the practice. Do you know someone who takes minutes for several boards and would spare 30 minutes to tell me how they work? A two-line introduction would be a great help.
>
> Regards,
> [Name]

## 7.3 Follow-up after an interview (sent only if they scored hot or warm)

> Dear [Name],
>
> Thank you for your time on [date]. It was very useful, particularly [one specific thing they said].
>
> As mentioned, we're taking three practices into a paid eight-week pilot: up to six meetings, with draft minutes delivered in your template and every decision linked to the recording. The draft terms are attached. They are clear about what is software and what is still done by hand.
>
> Would you like one of the places? I'm happy to walk through the terms by phone.
>
> Regards,
> [Name]

**Do not attach** the pilot terms until the data-processing addendum is ready (see 2.4).
