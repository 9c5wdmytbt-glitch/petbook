# 3. Buyer interview script (12 qualified interviews)

**Purpose:** find out what buyers **actually do and spend today**, not whether they like our idea. Ask about the last real meeting, not about "usually" or "would you". Do not describe the product until Part F.

**Length:** about 40 minutes, with a 10-minute demo at the end only if they qualify.

**Rule of thumb:** if you hear yourself explaining, stop and ask "tell me about the last time…".

## 3.1 Who counts as qualified (screen before booking)

All four must be true:

1. They prepare minutes themselves, or are accountable for minutes, for **at least 2 separate boards or committees**, or at least **4 formal meetings a month**.
2. The minutes are formal: board, committee, trustee or AGM, and signed or approved by a chair.
3. They decide on spend for the practice, or sit one step away from the person who does (name that person).
4. They are based in Botswana, or serve Botswana-registered entities.

If someone fails the screen, still have the conversation if it is offered, but record it as **unqualified**. It does not count towards the 12.

## 3.2 Script

### Part A: Context (5 minutes)

1. "How many organisations do you, or your team, take minutes for at the moment?"
2. "Roughly how many formal meetings is that a month? Board, committee, AGM, trustees?"
3. "Who in your team actually writes them?"

### Part B: The last meeting (10 minutes). This is the core of the interview.

4. "Think about the last set of board minutes you finished. Which meeting was it, and when?"
5. "Walk me through what happened from the moment the meeting ended to the moment the minutes were signed."
   Probe each step: "What did you use for that? How long did it take?"
6. "Was it recorded? If so, how, and who kept the recording? If not, why not?"
7. "How long did the first draft take? How many rounds of comments came back? From whom?"
8. "What did the chair or directors change?" (Listen for wrong decisions, missing names, tone, length.)
9. "Was anything in the minutes ever disputed later? What happened?"

### Part C: The cost today (5 minutes)

10. "Who does the hours? Roughly what would that person's time cost, or what do you charge the client for minute-taking?"
11. "Is minute-taking billed to the client, included in a retainer, or written off?"
    (This matters. If it is billed by the hour, saving time can *reduce* their revenue. Listen for that.)
12. "When there's a backlog, what gives? Late minutes, overtime, outsourcing?"

### Part D: What they've tried (5 minutes)

13. "Have you tried anything to speed this up? Transcription apps, Copilot, Otter, a board portal, dictation, templates?"
14. Follow up: "What happened? Do you still use it? Why or why not?"
15. "Do any of your clients use a board portal such as Diligent, BoardPro, Convene or AgendaWorx? Who writes the minutes for those?"

### Part E: Constraints and authority (5 minutes)

16. "If you wanted to record board meetings and send the audio to a service, who would have to agree?"
17. "What would a director or chair worry about?"
18. "When you last bought software or a service for the practice, who decided, and how long did it take?"
19. "Is there a budget line for this kind of thing, or would it come from client fees?"

### Part F: Demo and ask (10 minutes, qualified candidates only)

Say this **exactly**: "We're testing a service, not selling finished software. Today it's software plus a person checking the work. I'll show you sample output from a fictitious meeting we staged."

20. Show the demo (`demo/`). Then stay quiet. Ask: "What's wrong with it?" before asking "What's good?"
21. "If this landed in your inbox two days after your next board meeting, what would you still have to do before sending it to the chair?"
22. **Ask for the pilot. Don't hint at it:** "We're running eight-week paid pilots for three practices. It covers up to six meetings, delivered in your template, and costs US$600. Would you want one of the three places?"
23. If they hesitate: "What would have to be true for that to be an easy yes?" Then: "What would you expect to pay for a set of minutes done this way?" **Write down the first number they say.**

**Close:** "Who else should I speak to?"

## 3.3 Don'ts

1. Don't ask "Would you use…?" or "Would you pay for…?" before Part F. Everyone says yes.
2. Don't defend the idea. Write down the objection and move on.
3. Don't count compliments, "send me more information" or "let's talk next quarter" as evidence. Only money, a signed pilot, a named decision-maker introduction or a shared real minute template (shared with permission) counts.
4. Don't accept or listen to confidential client details. If they start naming clients and board matters, steer back to process.
